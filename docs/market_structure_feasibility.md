# Market shares on the Industry lens: feasibility and options

*7 November 2026 (DECISIONS E-125). Option 1 built the same day; option 4 to follow as a cross-check. For the owner's question: can the lens show, above each column, the share of the market held by entrants, by small and medium competitors and by oligopoly players, from ABS data?*

## Short answer

**Not as asked.** No public ABS source gives market shares (shares of sales) by firm type for an ANZSIC class, let alone a class in one state:

- **Entrants' share of sales** is not published at any level. It needs firm-level data (the ABS's BLADE, which is restricted).
- **Sales by business size** are published only by division, for Australia: ABS *Australian Industry* (8155.0), Table 5. Finance and insurance is not in that table.
- **Concentration** (the four largest firms' share of sales) is published only as division averages, to 2019: the RBA's Research Discussion Paper 2025-05 (Hambur and Freestone), computed from BLADE. Its class-level values are not published.

What *is* published for every class and state is the **number** of businesses by employment size, with entries and exits: ABS *Counts of Australian Businesses* (CABEE). Counts are not market shares.

## What the data we hold can show (worked example: cafes and restaurants, NSW)

| Column | Measure | Value | Source and level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Entrants | New businesses in 2024-25, as a share of those operating at the start of the year | 18.1% (3,481 of 19,205); exits 17.1% | CABEE, class × state |
| Small and medium competitors | Businesses with 1–199 employees | 75.9% of businesses (1–19: 68.3%; 20–199: 7.5%); non-employing 23.7% | CABEE, class × state, June 2025 |
| | Share of sales earned by businesses with fewer than 200 employees | 69.1% of Accommodation and food services sales in 2024-25 (0–4: 17.5%; 5–19: 20.9%; 20–199: 30.7%) | ABS 8155.0 Table 5, division, Australia |
| Largest players | Businesses with 200+ employees | 91, or 0.5% of businesses | CABEE, class × state |
| | Their share of sales | 30.9% of the division's sales | ABS 8155.0 Table 5, division, Australia |
| | Four largest firms' share of sales in a typical industry | 17.2% in 2019 (sales-weighted average over the division's 6 industries) | RBA RDP 2025-05, division |

Firms with 200+ employees are the largest firms, not necessarily oligopolists. Concentration (the top-four share) is the closer measure of oligopoly, but it is published only for divisions.

## Options

1. **Build now from public sources (recommended first step).** Above each column, show the measures in the table: CABEE counts and rates for the class and state, with sales shares by size and top-four concentration for the class's division. Add a "structural market conditions" chart:
   - the share of businesses by size (class and state) beside the share of sales by size (division);
   - entry and exit rates since 2007-08 (class and state);
   - the division's top-four share, 2002 to 2019.

   Every figure is labelled with its level (class or division) and year. All three sources are authoritative and CC BY 4.0, and already ingested by ALICIA. It answers "how fragmented or concentrated is this market?" at the finest public level. It does not give entrants' market share, and it gives sales shares only by division.
2. **Exact measures from BLADE through ABS DataLab.** An approved project could compute, by class (and possibly by state), three measures:
   - the share of sales held by young firms (entrants), by small and medium firms, and by large firms;
   - top-four shares and the Herfindahl index;
   - their trends.

   This is the authoritative route to what was asked. It needs an accredited researcher and organisation, a project approval, and ABS output checks; small classes may be suppressed. Timelines and any fees are set by the ABS.
3. **ABS custom tabulation.** Ask the ABS to tabulate *Australian Industry* sales by employment size for classes or subdivisions. This gives sales shares by size at a finer level, quicker than option 2, but still no entrants or concentration. It is charged.
4. **Regulators' market studies for the most concentrated industries.** ACCC inquiries and PC reports publish market shares for particular markets (supermarkets, airlines, banking, electricity and others). These can be added industry by industry with citations, alongside option 1. Coverage is patchy and the dates vary.
5. **Commercial industry reports (not recommended).** These include class-level market shares, but they are licensed, not public, and their methods are not authoritative.

## Recommendation

Option 1 now. Pursue option 2 if the project needs entrants' market share or class-level concentration. Add option 4 for the industries where the register already flags concentration (banking, supermarkets, airlines, pharmacy).
