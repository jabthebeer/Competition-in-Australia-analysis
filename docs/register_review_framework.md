# Register review under the productivity framework (28 October 2026)

> Phase 3 of the owner's change of direction (28 October 2026; `alicia/DECISIONS.md` E-85 to E-88). Claude Code's review of the register against the [productivity impact framework](productivity_framework.md), written for the owner and Cowork. The tables are generated by `uv run python scripts/register_summary.py` (in `alicia/`) from the register as committed on 28 October 2026; the judgements are desk research and every assessment is a draft until a person has checked it ([review sheet](review/framework_assessments_review.csv)).

## 1. What changed

The register was built to find reforms that could be **evaluated** with public data: changes adopted by several states at different dates, with business counts or prices to compare. The framework widens the question to **anything that affects productivity and competition**, so the register now holds three kinds of item:

| Kind | Marked against | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Reform | the settings it replaced or would replace | ride-share legalisation, the non-compete ban (proposed) |
| Existing setting | the same market without it | residential stamp duty, payroll tax, foreign investment screening |
| Non-legislative barrier | the same market without it | supermarket concentration, non-compete clauses in contracts |

Every item has a mark from −2 to +2 on eight drivers, an overall mark (their sum), a main driver, a reach and a confidence rating.

## 3. What the review found

1. **The reforms are a skewed sample.** They were chosen because they could be evaluated, which favoured state-by-state adoptions of national schemes: health practitioner registration, the 2022 building code, NDIS registration, pharmacist UTI prescribing and ride-share have eight records each. The 171 reform items are 59 policy units. Most are harmonisation or deregulation: 105 of 171 reform items are positive overall and 47 negative. On their own they would suggest competition policy has mostly moved the right way; they say little about why competition may be weakening.
2. **Where reforms are negative, regulatory burden is usually why.** 26 of the 47 negative reform items have burden as their main driver: the 2022 building code, NDIS provider registration, container deposit schemes and building practitioner registration. This matches the Productivity Commission's 2025 finding that the cumulative burden of regulation is a brake on dynamism.
3. **Three drivers were barely covered.** Before phase 4, only one negative item (coastal shipping) had trade and market integration as its main driver, capital deepening was the main driver of four items, and innovation of three. The register had nothing on taxes that bear on mobility and growth (stamp duty, payroll tax), on openness to trade and foreign investment, or on market structure.
4. **The settings and barriers carry the explanation.** All 27 items added in phase 4 are negative, and eight of the fourteen policy units in the table above are among them. They point to the main reasons competition may be weak: concentrated markets in groceries, banking, aviation and digital platforms; taxes that discourage moving home and growing a firm; restraints on workers moving; and remaining barriers to trade and foreign investment.
5. **Some records need reframing or follow-up.**
   - Queensland's non-adoption of automatic mutual recognition is now an existing setting (it was a reform record with no commencement).
   - `cth-pharmacy-location-rules-2018` marks the removal of the rules' sunset. The standing rules themselves and the state pharmacy ownership laws deserve their own setting records (see §4).
   - The non-compete ban, the revitalised National Competition Policy and the EPBC reforms are not yet in force; their marks depend on implementation and should be revisited when they commence.
   - Family members repeat one assessment with jurisdiction notes. Questions of "how much" should use policy units, not items; `register_summary.py` reports both.
   - Several narrow reforms are of little productivity consequence (Boxing Day trading, the Adelaide CBD precinct, small-bar licences, the gaming venue models, the CTP schemes). They stay in the register for completeness; the reach filter on the menu separates them.
   - The econometric fields (`comparison`, `confounders`, `price_series`) are kept for the archive and are optional for new items.
6. **Evidence is thin.** 28 items are rated high confidence, mostly where a reform removed a cap by design or an official inquiry has documented the barrier. The archived econometric evaluations found no detectable effects, which is consistent with effects too small to see in aggregate data rather than with no effect. The parked literature review for each record (P-1 in `parked_items.md`) is the most direct way to raise confidence.
