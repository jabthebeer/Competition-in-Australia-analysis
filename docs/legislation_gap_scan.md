# Legislation gap scan: competition and productivity reforms missing from the register

**Date:** 18 October 2026.

**Method:**
- Four research passes, each against the 69 records in `regulation-register/records/`:
  - Commonwealth market regulation;
  - state occupational, retail, liquor and fuel rules;
  - state housing, infrastructure, energy and training markets;
  - productivity, tax and labour-market rules.
- Each pass looked for reforms from about 2005 to 2026 that bear on competition or productivity and have no record.

**Status:** candidates only. No register records have been created.

**Verification key (Ver.):**
- **V:** date confirmed on an official legislation register or agency page.
- **P:** partly confirmed (official media release, search extract of an official page, or agency page plus secondary source).
- **U:** unverified.

Before any candidate becomes a record, its dates go through the usual check (`alicia dates`) against the legislation registers.

## 1. Key findings

1. **Whole reform families are missing:**
   - price transparency (fuel);
   - scope-of-practice reforms (pharmacists);
   - care markets (NDIS, aged care, childcare subsidy);
   - financial-services competition (open banking, bank levy, card surcharging);
   - small-business tax and insolvency thresholds;
   - infrastructure privatisation (ports, electricity networks);
   - planning and zoning;
   - the energy retail framework;
   - contestable training markets;
   - insurance market design;
   - gaming market structure;
   - agricultural marketing.
2. **The most evaluable new candidates** are state reforms adopted at different dates with public outcome data:
   - fuel price reporting (six states, 2016–2025);
   - pharmacist UTI prescribing (eight jurisdictions, 2020–2024);
   - the National Energy Customer Framework (2012–2015);
   - small-bar licences (2007–2013);
   - e-conveyancing mandates (2018–2023);
   - state adoption of the 2022 building code (2023–2025).
3. **Fuel price transparency could be evaluated next with data already in the pipeline.** Automotive fuel prices are in the ABS CPI by capital city, and WA (always reporting) and the ACT (never mandatory) give fixed comparisons.
4. **A second large group is keyed to firm-size or turnover thresholds:**
   - company tax rates;
   - instant asset write-off;
   - small business restructuring;
   - the R&D incentive;
   - right to disconnect;
   - card surcharging rules;
   - the major bank levy.

   These suit discontinuity and bunching designs, but mostly need firm-level data (BLADE, ATO), which cannot be published here.
5. **Much of the 2024–26 agenda is not yet in force:**
   - the non-compete ban (exposure draft, intended from 2027);
   - the National Competition Policy reforms;
   - gas reservation;
   - the surcharge ban from 1 October 2026.

   These are candidates for plans registered before they commence.

## 6. Suggested next steps

1. **Add Tier 1 as draft register records first.** Start with fuel price reporting, pharmacist UTI prescribing and the National Energy Customer Framework, checking each date with `alicia dates`.
2. **Run each new family through the Phase 3 power study before planning:**
   - fuel price reporting with CPI automotive fuel, against the other capitals;
   - the energy framework with CPI electricity;
   - small bars, e-conveyancing and the building code with CABEE entry and exit.
3. **Add a "pending" status** to the register for reforms announced but not in force, so they can be pre-registered before they start.
4. **Data to request or connect:**
   - MBS and PBS statistics by state (pharmacist prescribing);
   - AIP fuel prices;
   - APRA monthly bank statistics;
   - NCVER provider data;
   - Department of Education childcare data (manual download).

## Limits

- **Dates.** They were checked by research passes using official pages where reachable. Several official sites blocked direct fetching (ATO, TGA, NDIS, AER, NCVER, NSW legislation), so "P" often means a search extract of the official page. Nothing here is a finding.
- **Hypotheses.** Effect directions are first hypotheses, to be tested.
