# Economies most similar to Australia: proposed peer group

Generated by `alicia international peers` from OECD STAN 2025 and World Bank WDI data. Status: **proposed core group, awaiting owner approval (decision D-1)**.

## Proposal

Core peer group (6 economies): **Canada, Chile, United Kingdom, New Zealand, France, Spain**. The OECD median is shown alongside as a second comparator, and results are checked against the fixed list (Canada, New Zealand, Norway, United Kingdom, United States) and with each peer left out.

Peers without OECD employer business demography, and where their entry and exit rates would come from: Chile (no national source identified yet). Such comparisons need harmonising to the OECD definitions and carry a lower comparability rating.

## Method

Four distances from Australia, each averaged over a window of years:

- **Industry structure**: the dissimilarity index of value-added shares across the common industry groups (half the sum of absolute differences in shares, from 0 for identical structures to 1 for no overlap). Shares exclude the imputed rent of owner-occupied dwellings, which has no businesses or workers.
- **Income**: the gap in log GDP per head at purchasing power parity.
- **Size**: the gap in log population.
- **Openness**: the gap in log trade (exports plus imports) as a share of GDP.

Each distance is divided by its typical size between two OECD economies (the root mean square over all pairs of members), so the four are in the same units, and the score is their weighted sum: industry structure 1/2 and the other three 1/6 each, because structure matters most for comparing industry statistics. Lower is more similar. Variants change the weights, drop one distance at a time and move the window, to show how stable the ranking is; the proposed core group is the six most similar economies, which the owner approves before any benchmark is computed.

Shares are averaged over the window's years in which every group is available. Where fewer than three such years exist (mostly because the country does not split out imputed rent, which real estate needs), real estate is left out of that country's comparison and the shares of both countries are taken over the other groups. Where a country does not split out rental and leasing, it stays with professional and administrative services. The ranking describes similarity only; it says nothing about which economy performs better.

Typical distances between two OECD economies (the scales): structure 0.201, log income 0.587, log population 2.169, log trade share 0.780.

## Limits

- Similarity is measured on economy-wide features. Two economies can be alike overall and differ within an industry (for example, the kind of mining).
- GDP per head for Ireland and Luxembourg is inflated by multinational profits and cross-border workers, so their income distance overstates how different living standards are.
- Value-added shares at current prices move with commodity prices, which matters for Australia, Norway, Canada and Chile; the earlier and recent windows show how much.
- The weights are a judgement. The variants show which economies are close on any reasonable choice.
