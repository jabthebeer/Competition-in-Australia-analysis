# Quality check of the state and territory register (24 November 2026)

**Covers:**
- the 861 state and territory requirements in `regulation-register/requirements/` (32 of them Victorian);
- the 7 state and territory catalogues that are fully called (`data/processed/legislation/{nsw,qld,wa,sa,tas,act,nt}_catalogue.csv`);
- the gap log, `regulation-register/requirement-gaps.json`.

**When:** after the last 'to draft' cells were drafted (DECISIONS E-351), as batch D-11 of the state plan ([`plan_states.md`](plan_states.md)). Victoria is left out: its catalogue waits for the owner's uploads.

**Who:** the same drafting process that wrote the register. This is not an independent review. Every requirement is still a draft until the AI checking process checks it (DECISIONS E-145).

## Part 1: automated checks

**Locators.** Each locator was compared with the nearest section heading before its quote.
- 4 duty quotes cited the wrong section: SA cemeteries (s 9(1), not s 8(2)), SA centralised booking services (s 29(2), not s 28(2)), the Tasmanian waste levy (s 31(4), not s 32(4)) and an ACT property developer quote (s 6(2)(a), not s 3(a)).
- Most administering-body locators had been guessed by the linking script from the last number before the quote, and many were wrong. 102 body quotes that are definitions now cite their interpretation section, dictionary or schedule. 45 other body locators were corrected by reading the heading. 48 locators set by hand were kept.
- About 200 body locators in NSW, Queensland, the ACT and the NT could not be checked. Their texts do not keep section headings in a form a script can read. The quotes are exact; only the section numbers are unverified (open item in [`plan_legislation_links.md`](plan_legislation_links.md)).

**Descriptions.** 15 descriptions gave rates, periods or dates that no quote contains, such as the ACT training levy's 0.2% and WA's '24 hours from 6 am'. They were trimmed to what the quotes say.

**Template carry-over.** 5 titles said 'NSW coastal waters' and 4 reach conditions named NSW in another state's requirement. They were fixed.

**Cut quotes.** 30 quotes began or ended in the middle of a word. They were widened to whole words.

**Read and left as they are.** 13 quotes serve more than one family in a state, because one provision covers both (for example, the ACT's motor dealer and motor repairer licences).

## Part 2: sample of requirements

Each of the 30 was read for whether its description, reach, triggers and framework marks follow from its quotes.

**Passed as drafted:** 24.

**Fixed, with the same text in the rest of the family:** 6.
- The Tasmanian high-roller casino mark cited 'a cap of 2 casino licences in NSW'. The same reason was fixed in the ACT, NT and SA casino records. The NT quote sets no cap, so its mark was lowered.
- The Queensland rental bond mark named NSW's Secretary. Fixed in 6 rental bond records.
- The Queensland tobacco marks described the old notice scheme, not the licence the quote now requires.
- The SA and Tasmanian waste transport summaries claimed that each load is tracked. No quote shows this; fixed in 7 records.
- The NSW mine licensed activities description listed more than its quotes cover.

A scan of every state summary and mark reason then found more of the same: 6 point-of-consumption tax reasons citing NSW's 15% rate, 6 hazardous chemicals summaries describing WA's site licence, and 10 marks with a 14-day wait or a 5-year renewal that no quote shows. All were fixed.

Figures from published studies are left in some marks: Treasury's 72 cents per dollar for transfer duty and the Reserve Bank's zoning premia for 4 capital cities. They have no evidence entry yet.

## Part 3: sample of N calls

**The sample.** 2 of 40 were wrong:
- the NSW Adoption Act 2000: only the Secretary or an accredited adoption service provider may provide adoption services (s 11(2));
- the NSW Stock Medicines Act 1989: owners must keep treated stock out of the food chain during the withholding period. It supports the agvet chemical use requirement.

Both had matched the screening pattern. The misses were judgement calls, not pattern failures.

**The re-scan.** Every state N law was searched again for licensing offences aimed at business activities. 235 laws had such wording. Most are public bodies, land dealings by statutory authorities, or rules that bind everyone. The rest led to these changes.

| Change | Laws |
|---|---|
| 7 new families, each drafted for the state where it was found | NSW adoption service accreditation (s 11(2)) and wool, hide and skin dealer licences (s 7(1)); WA funeral director licences for each cemetery (Cemeteries Act 1986 s 14(1)) and pet shop approvals for selling dogs (Dog Act 1976 s 38B); SA foster care agency licences (s 98); ACT X 18+ film sale licences (s 22(4)); NT stevedore licences (Ports Management Act 2015 s 56(1)) |
| 4 'not found' cells drafted | ACT private psychiatric facilities (Mental Health Act 2015 s 224(1)); NT crematoria (Burial and Cremation Act 2022 s 124(1)), prohibited weapons dealers (Weapons Control Act 2001 s 6) and trade in parks (Territory Parks and Wildlife Conservation By-laws 1984) |
| 8 calls changed to L | For example the NSW Stock Medicines Act, the Tasmanian commercial dive and shellfish rules, and the NT Public and Environmental Health Regulations 2014 |
| 9 calls changed to 'worth looking at again' (Y) | For example WA's firewood moisture permits and NSW's wharf authorisations |
| 4 N reasons rewritten | NT council by-laws on stall and sign permits, left to the council phase |

The other 7 jurisdictions are logged as 'not searched' for each new family (49 cells).

**Earlier in the phase.** TD-3 found that 21 SA catalogue rows had been screened against another law's text. An Act and a regulation can share a register number, and the lookup took the first file (DECISIONS E-352). They were re-screened.

**What this says about the screening.** The screening pattern finds licensing offences well. The calls that were wrong rested on judgement: duties on charities and other organisations, or on small industries such as wool dealing, were called N as if they bound no business. Phase G should sample N calls again, especially those with a licensing pattern match.

## Part 4: gap log

The gap log has 660 cells:
- 490 not found;
- 121 blocked, mostly Victorian laws waiting for upload, and 4 elsewhere;
- 49 not searched, for the D-11d families.

The 81 'not found' cells in families drafted in 5 or more jurisdictions were read by their notes. Most are genuine absences, such as the ACT having no mining or petroleum law. 9 notes left room for doubt and were read again in the law. 2 were wrong:
- Tasmania's Racing Regulation and Integrity Act 2024 requires bookmakers to be registered (s 132(1));
- the NT Building Act 1993 requires residential building insurance or a fidelity certificate before prescribed residential building work.

The other 7 stand. For example, the Queensland Casino Control Act 1982 licenses casino staff, but casino licences come from agreement Acts.

## Limits

- The samples are small: 30 of 861 state requirements (3.5%) and 40 of 5,161 state N calls (under 1%).
- The checks were run by the process that drafted the register, so they share its blind spots.
- The re-scan searched for licensing offences only. A duty worded another way, such as a levy or a duty to notify, would be missed. Laws with no text held (2,061 set aside by title triage) were not re-scanned.
- Victoria is not covered.
- NSW and SA quotes come from the corpus copy, at the version each locator shows. The NSW register refuses automated downloads, so later NSW changes are not checked.
