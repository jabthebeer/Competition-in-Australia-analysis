# How Australia compares: benchmarks against comparable economies

Generated by `alicia international benchmark` (phase P3 of the [benchmarking plan](forward_plan_international_benchmarking.md), E-106). Peers: the core group (Canada, United Kingdom, New Zealand, France, Spain, Italy). Inputs and definitions: [methods note](methods_international_benchmarks.md). Tables: `data/processed/international/benchmarks.parquet` (summary) and `benchmark_years.parquet` (each year). Cross-checked in R: `analysis/crosscheck_international_benchmarks.R`.

## Rule

For each group, measure and window, each year counts when Australia and at least four of the six core peers (E-103) have a figure. In each counted year the engine takes the core peers' median, mean, standard deviation and interquartile range (25th to 75th percentile, interpolated between order statistics as R's default type 7 does) and notes whether Australia is below, within or above that range. Over the window it reports Australia's average, the peers' average median and range, the gap to the median in points, as a share of the median and in peer standard deviations, Australia's rank among the seven, and how the gap moved between the two windows.

A gap is flagged (decision D-8) when all of these hold: Australia is on the same side of the peer range in more than half the counted years, with at least two counted years; that still holds with each core peer left out in turn (each variant needing three peers a year); Australia's average gap to the median over the window points the same way (a check that a few extreme years do not hide the opposite); and Australia's figure is rated medium or high in every counted year. Being outside the range on either side counts; which side is a concern is for the policy menu (phase P4). A low-rated comparison is shown with its status but never flagged.

Windows: 2015-2019 and 2022-2024 for business demography and productivity (2020 and 2021 are left out, being distorted by the pandemic and JobKeeper); for regulation, the two latest product market regulation vintages (2018 and 2023) and 2021-2025 for services trade and FDI restrictiveness. Two further comparisons are reported but do not enter the flag: the plan's fixed list (Canada, New Zealand, Norway, UK, US) and the median of all OECD members.

A flag says where to look, not why a gap exists; it is descriptive and identifies no cause.

## Limits

- Six peers make wide ranges: one country can move the median, hence the leave-one-out test.
- The 2022-2024 window is thin for business demography: the peers' data end in 2022 or 2023, and the UK's in 2018.
- Australia's gross birth, death and churn rates are rated low (E-105, accepted by the owner, who may revisit it: `GROSS_RATES_RATING` in `international/harmonise.py`), so gaps in them are shown, not flagged.
- Regulation indicators are national; a group's sector indicators cover only part of it. The business economy is MIN, MAN, CON, TRD, TRN, ACC, ICT, RES, PRO, EDU, HLT, OTH.
- A flag is descriptive: it says where to look, not what causes the gap or whether a policy would close it.
