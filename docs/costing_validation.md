## Notes

- **L2.** The shock is the PC's exactly (1.6 × 0.5); the difference is Layer B's aggregation.
- **L1.** The PC applies 0.24% in 24 service industries and 0.14% in the rest; it does not list the 24, so the tool uses the midpoint everywhere (A-P21).
- **H5.** No lever covers the PC's telehealth chain, so its shock is entered as custom shocks.
- **B7tar.** The tariff removal itself works through prices and the terms of trade and is not costed by formula (F-C10-02). The isolation assumes the PC model is linear in the MFP shock; PC National's linearity tests (B9, D4hl, L2) find results within about 1% of proportional. The gap is the multiplier on an economy-wide MFP shock: 1.91 in the tool (1/(1 − α), α from the input-output tables) against 1.60 in the PC's runs. The back-test's MFP cases shock single industries, so this case is outside them.
- **TAX25.** The models are CGE models in which employment changes by −0.1% to +0.4%; the tool's closure fixes employment, so it sits in the lower part of their range. It applies the rate to all capital in the industries reached, unincorporated businesses included.
- **Not reproduced: the PC's 2025 company tax package** (a 20% rate below $1 billion of revenue, 28% above, and a 5% net cash-flow tax). The brief has one company tax rate and no cash-flow tax, so the package cannot be entered; its modelled GDP gain (0.4% to 0.7% in the PC's two models) is context only.
- Layer B's back-test against every PC National productivity simulation is in [`costing_calibration.md`](costing_calibration.md).
